UAV Swarm Spoofing Defense

StatusActive · preprint on arXiv SettingSimulation — ArduPilot SITL and Gazebo OutputRigid-Covert GNSS Spoofing of UAV Swarms

The blind spot

Cooperative swarm defenses usually work by cross-checking what GNSS reports against the geometry the drones can measure among themselves. If a reported position drifts away from where the inter-drone ranges say a drone should be, something is wrong.

That check has a structural hole. If an attacker moves the entire formation slowly and together, every pairwise distance stays exactly as it was. The discrepancy the defense looks for is zero — not small, not below threshold, but zero by construction. No amount of tuning closes it, because it is a gauge freedom rather than a detection failure.

Restoring the frame

What relative measurements can never supply is an absolute frame. Inter-drone ranges determine the swarm's shape perfectly well; they say nothing about where that shape sits in the world. So the recovery works in two halves: rebuild the shape from ranges, then root that shape on a small number of references that do not come from GNSS.

The alignment step has to survive a lying reference, so it is fitted robustly rather than by least squares — a compromised anchor is outvoted instead of averaged in. Once the frame is recovered, absolute positions follow for every drone, including the ones carrying no anchor of their own.

Where it stops working

The interesting part of this problem is not that a defense exists, but that its limits can be stated precisely. There is a drift-dependent floor below which a slow enough attack is indistinguishable from ordinary anchor drift. Anchor placement matters: collinear references leave the frame underdetermined. And if a majority of anchors are compromised, the trust root itself is gone and nothing downstream can repair it.

Every result here is simulation-based. No RF spoofing hardware and no physical swarm were involved.
