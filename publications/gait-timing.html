Estimating Gait Phase and Gait-Event Timing from Hip-Angle Sensing Alone: Feasibility and Limits on a Commercial Hip Exoskeleton

Working title. The final title will be posted once the manuscript is submitted.

AuthorsM.S. Park, H. Kim (Corresponding Author) StatusManuscript in preparation · to be submitted, 2026 KeywordsProsthetics and exoskeletons · wearable robotics · gait event detection · gait phase estimation · gait analysis

Abstract

Hip-exoskeleton assistance must be delivered at the right point in the gait cycle, which requires both a continuous gait phase and the timing of the main foot-contact events — heel strike (HS), foot flat (FF), heel off (HO), and toe off (TO). Existing four-event estimators, however, rely on plantar-pressure sensors, distal IMUs, or multi-joint kinematics, all of which add sensing that a hip exoskeleton does not have. Whether bilateral hip encoders alone can recover both the phase and all four events — and where this proximal-only sensing reaches its limit — has not been established.

Using only the sagittal angles of the two hip encoders and the angular velocities derived from them, we train separate temporal convolutional networks for phase and for event estimation, evaluate them subject-independently on two public datasets, transfer the trained models to a commercial hip exoskeleton scored against synchronized video, and analyze the hip-motion cue behind each event.

Hip-only sensing reached a phase RMSE of 2.84 %, with median absolute event-timing errors of 5, 15, 40, and 10 ms for HS, FF, HO, and TO; on the device, the selected configuration reached F1 scores of 0.99, 0.95, 0.94, and 0.96 at a ±200 ms tolerance. These results show that the bilateral hip encoders already present on such devices can provide both continuous gait phase and the four principal foot-contact events without any additional distal sensing, supporting sensor-minimal gait-state estimation for hip-exoskeleton control.
