Abstract

Autonomous mobility is evolving toward perceiving the surrounding environment and estimating the position and velocity of objects based on LiDAR and Radar. Concurrently, the significance of security threats targeting active sensors and perception processing architectures is growing. Security for LiDAR and Radar-based perception systems encompasses issues such as physical signal manipulation, object perception disruption, and the induction of errors in sensor fusion and tracking processes. This paper reorganizes research on attacks and defenses for LiDAR and Radar-based autonomous perception systems, focusing on the intervention points within the perception process, and compares and analyzes related studies under a consistent framework. Finally, based on the analysis results, this paper proposes future research directions for LiDAR/Radar perception security.

Why reorganize the field

Work on LiDAR and radar security is usually catalogued by attack name — spoofing, jamming, relay, adversarial examples. That grouping places methods side by side that have little in common once you ask which part of the system they actually touch, and it makes defenses hard to compare, because two papers that both claim to stop spoofing may be operating at completely different stages of the pipeline.

This review reorganizes the same body of work around a different axis: where in the perception process the attack or defense intervenes. Under that organization, the assumption a technique breaks, and therefore the class of defense that can possibly catch it, is what determines where it belongs.

The three-layer attack surface

The paper models the perception pipeline as a three-layer attack surface and traces how the impact of a threat propagates across those layers.

LayerWhat it coversWhat an attacker changes there
Input formation The physical return and its conversion into a point cloud or range-Doppler map Physical signal manipulation: point distortion, removal, injection, spoofed range and velocity
Object perception Detection and classification over that representation Object perception disruption: false positives, false negatives, misclassification
State integration Fusion across sensors and tracking across time Induced errors in fusion and tracking: pose and velocity error, latency-induced tracking error

One comparison framework for every study

Rather than describing each paper in its own terms, every surveyed work is entered into the same set of columns, which is what makes cross-study comparison possible:

  • Intervention point — the exact place in the pipeline the technique acts on
  • Mechanism — how it acts there
  • Target — what it tries to affect or protect
  • Perception error — the resulting failure, stated as false positive, false negative, misclassification, or range and velocity offset
  • Experiment type — physical, simulation, or dataset, so that results validated on hardware are not silently equated with simulation-only claims
  • Vehicle behavior impact — what the error means for the vehicle
  • Sensor type — LiDAR, Radar, or fusion

Eight summary tables apply this framework across the layers: attacks at input formation (LiDAR and Radar treated separately) and at object perception, then defenses at input formation, at object perception, at multi-sensor state integration, and at LiDAR-Radar fusion.

Coverage

Twenty-nine studies published between 2017 and 2026 are surveyed, drawn from venues including NDSS, USENIX Security, ACM CCS, CVPR, MobiSys, SenSys, and VehicleSec. The distribution is heavily weighted toward recent work, which is itself a finding about how fast this area is moving.
