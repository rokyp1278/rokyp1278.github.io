Maritime SAR Ship-Intrusion Alert near the NLL

Year2026 ContextDefense AI course project StackSAR imagery · YOLOv8n-OBB · synthetic data pipeline

Why radar

This project comes out of night coastal-guard duty at Yeonpyeong Island. The watch there depends on optical sensors — Hawkeye and TOD — and those sensors fail exactly when the watch matters most. Sea fog, foul weather, and darkness all blind them, and a vessel approaching the Northern Limit Line under those conditions is the case the equipment handles worst.

Synthetic aperture radar does not care about fog or daylight. The question this project asks is whether a SAR-based watch can not only see the vessels but decide, on its own, which of them warrant an alert.

Building the data that does not exist

No public dataset contains SAR sequences of vessels crossing a maritime boundary, so the approach tracks had to be synthesized. The pipeline starts from HRSID and works in three steps:

  • Cut out — ship instances are extracted along their COCO segmentation polygons, so each vessel comes away with its true SAR signature rather than a rectangular crop.
  • Inpaint — the sea left behind is filled in, removing the vessel from its original position without leaving a visible hole.
  • Re-composite — the extracted vessels are placed back along linear approach paths toward a drawn NLL, producing a sequence in which each ship advances frame by frame.

Detection and alerting

Detection uses YOLOv8n-OBB, which returns an oriented bounding box rather than an axis-aligned one. That orientation is what makes the alert possible: a rectangle tells you a ship is present, but an oriented box tells you which way its bow is pointing.

Three quantities are derived per vessel per frame — bow heading, range to the line, and closing speed relative to it — and combined into a five-level intrusion alert, so that a vessel running parallel to the boundary is not treated the same as one bearing down on it.

Summary

  • Motivation — optical watch (Hawkeye, TOD) blinded by fog, weather, and darkness
  • Data — HRSID SAR imagery; synthetic approach sequences via polygon cut-out and inpainting
  • Detection — YOLOv8n-OBB oriented bounding boxes
  • Alerting — bow heading, range, and closing speed vs. NLL, mapped to a five-level alert
