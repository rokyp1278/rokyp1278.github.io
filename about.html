What I work on

I work on the security of systems that sense the physical world. The common thread across my projects is that a robot, a vehicle, or a drone has to trust what its sensors report — and that trust is where these systems break. I am interested in finding the point where a sensing pipeline stops being verifiable, showing concretely what an attacker gains there, and then building the minimal extra reference that restores trust.

That question has taken three forms so far. In autonomous driving, it meant classifying LiDAR and radar attacks by where in the perception pipeline they intervene. In UAV swarms, it meant proving that defenses built on relative geometry are structurally blind to an attack that moves the entire formation rigidly, and then recovering absolute position from a few trusted anchors. In wearable robotics, it meant reverse-engineering a commercial exoskeleton to read the sensor stream its firmware does not expose, and asking how much of the walking state that minimal signal can actually reconstruct.
